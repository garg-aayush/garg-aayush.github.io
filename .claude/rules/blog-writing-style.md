# Blog Posts: Writing Style

## Tone
- Write in first person, conversational tone, as if explaining something to someone one-on-one
- Keep the writing natural and not overly polished. It is okay to have minor grammatical imperfections; in Aayush's text, fix only clear typos such as a doubled word.
- Avoid AI hype language, gloating, or marketing-style adjectives. Keep reflections grounded in personal experience
- Own the work and give reasons in plain words ("I have made the agents global so that..."). Address the reader as "you" for advice ("Note, you also need to...").
- Keep Aayush's hedges ("based on my experience", "ideally", "maybe"). Never write a first-person claim he has not made, such as what was tested or why a tool was chosen; ask.

## Grammar
- Keep commas sparse: one after a sentence opener ("Currently,", "In this case,"), usually none before "and", "but", "so" or "which", and no Oxford comma. Do not add commas Aayush would not write.
- No contractions: "cannot", "do not", "I have".
- Prefer short sentences. Join two clauses with a plain "and" or "so", and split anything longer rather than chaining clauses with colons and parentheses.
- Ranges use a hyphen: 70-75%, 8-10 people.

## Content
- Keep posts short: only what others can use. A few short sections, paragraphs of two to four sentences, asides in a blockquote, and at most one bold key sentence.
- Cut what the reader does not need: mechanism detail where practical advice will do, meta asides ("more on this below") and recap lists that repeat earlier sections.
- No "X, not Y" one-liners such as "speed bumps, not a security boundary".
- Check claims about other tools against their docs or release notes, and name the version tested.

## Formatting
- Do not use em or en dashes
- Product and model names are plain text (OpenCode, GLM-5.3-Flash); code formatting is for commands, paths, config keys and ids such as `glm/coder-glm`.
- Emoji and smileys sparingly, where they fit the tone; the sentence still ends with a period, as in ":)."
- Keep titles straightforward and descriptive, not clickbait or overly dramatic (e.g. "Building Browser Tools with Claude Code" not "I Built 14 Tools Without Writing Code")
- Do not mention specific counts in titles or descriptions, they become stale as things change
- Screenshot placeholders use HTML comments: `<!-- Screenshot: description -->`
